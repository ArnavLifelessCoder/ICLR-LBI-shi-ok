\begin{abstract}
Interpretability-based safety often assumes that a concept which can be read off
a model's activations can also be controlled through the same representation: if
a linear probe detects deception or refusal, steering along that direction should
suppress the behavior. We test this assumption. For ten concepts across four
instruction-tuned models (1.5B to 9B) we measure two quantities separately:
\emph{readability}, the held-out AUROC of a linear probe, and
\emph{controllability}, the area under a steering dose-response curve up to a
preregistered fluency ceiling. Across 40 concept-model points the two are
uncorrelated (Spearman $0.14$, 95\% CI $[-0.24, 0.51]$): concepts that a probe
recovers at AUROC $1.0$ can resist all six steering interventions we apply,
while an identical pipeline moves a sentiment control. We then test the natural first-order explanation, that a
concept is steerable to the extent its direction projects into the model's
output-effective (unembedding) subspace, and find it \emph{inverted}: the
concepts most aligned with that subspace are the least steerable rather than the
most (partial Spearman $-0.45$, 95\% CI $[-0.72, -0.04]$, conditioning on
readability). We suggest that this overlap indexes how lexical a concept is
rather than how strongly behavior depends on it. Two implications follow for
interpretability-based safety: probe accuracy is not evidence that a
representation affords control, and the standard geometric account of when
steering should succeed has the wrong sign at this scale. We release the
datasets, probes, steering harness, and gap-map data.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A common premise in interpretability-based safety is that detection is a step
toward control. If a linear probe recovers a concept such as deception, harmful
intent, or sycophancy from the residual stream, the same direction is assumed to
provide a means of intervention. This premise underlies much of the
activation-steering and representation-engineering literature, in which a
difference-of-means direction is estimated and then added back to modify
behavior.

We measure two properties of each concept separately. \emph{Readability} is how
well a linear probe detects the concept from activations. \emph{Controllability}
is how much steering along the concept direction changes behavior, measured as
the area under a dose-response curve up to the point at which fluency degrades.
Plotting every concept-model pair in the readability-controllability plane, we
refer to the high-readability, low-controllability region as the danger zone.

Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item A measurement of readability against controllability for ten concepts
  across four instruction-tuned model families (Section~\ref{sec:results}). The
  two axes are uncorrelated on this data.
  \item A robustness protocol that separates ``unsteerable under the interventions
  we tried'' from ``immovable.'' A concept is only called immovable if it
  resists all six of single- and multi-layer activation addition, clamping,
  directional ablation, and steering on both the probe-weight and RepE
  reading-vector directions (Section~\ref{sec:method}).
  \item A preregistered test of the first-order geometric account of the gap.
  Controllability falls, rather than rises, as the concept direction aligns with
  the model's output-effective subspace (Section~\ref{sec:inverted}). We discuss
  a possible mechanism.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\textbf{Activation steering.} Difference-of-means and contrastive directions are
the standard tools for reading and moving concepts. Activation addition
\citep{turner2023actadd}, its contrastive-pairs refinement
\citep{panickssery2023caa}, inference-time intervention \citep{li2023iti} and
representation engineering \citep{zou2023repe} all read a direction and add it
back to change behavior. We use these methods rather than compare against them.

\textbf{The refusal direction.} Refusal is mediated by a single direction that
can be ablated \citep{arditi2024refusal}. \citet{joad2026refusal} find that
refusal and non-compliance span eleven categories occupying geometrically
distinct directions, yet steering along any of them produces nearly identical
refusal trade-offs, so the directions act as a shared one-dimensional control. Together these results suggest that
readability and controllability may decouple, but they concern refusal alone. We
extend the measurement to ten concepts and four models and test a geometric
predictor of the gap.

\textbf{Detection versus control.} \citet{galeone2026perfect} report a case in which
fake entities are detected with perfect linear separability while the detection
direction is nearly orthogonal to the direction that controls the behavior, and
show that the gap persists across four models from three families. They leave a systematic
comparison of detection against control, and a predictor of the gap, as future
work.

\textbf{Probing and what it licenses.} Linear probes on intermediate
activations date to \citet{alain2016probes}, and their interpretation has been
contested since. \citet{hewitt2019control} show that probe accuracy is
uninformative without a control task, and \citet{belinkov2022probing} surveys the
resulting methodological limits. Closest to our question,
\citet{elazar2021amnesic} argue that probing accuracy does not establish that a
model uses the probed property, and test use by removing the property and
measuring the behavioral consequence. That work removes information and asks
whether behavior degrades; we add information along a direction and ask whether
behavior moves. The removal operation itself has a literature
(\citealp{ravfogel2020inlp}; \citealp{belrose2023leace}), and directional ablation
is one of the six interventions in our robustness battery.

\textbf{Causal intervention.} Editing activations to test what a component
does is standard practice, from causal tracing and weight editing
\citep{meng2022rome} to the activation-patching methodology literature, which
documents how much the reported conclusion depends on the metric and the
corruption used \citep{zhang2023patching}. Our interventions are of this family,
applied to a fixed direction rather than to a located component.

\textbf{Limits of steering.} \citet{tan2024steering} report that steerability
varies substantially across inputs and that steering vectors can be brittle to
prompt changes. Their variation is across inputs within a concept; ours is
across concepts, and we pair it with a detection measurement on the same
concepts, which is what makes the dissociation visible.

\textbf{The linear representation hypothesis} \citep{park2024linear} motivates
treating concepts as directions and reading them with linear probes, which is the encoder half of our
measurement. Nothing in it ties the between-class separation a probe exploits to
the downstream causal sensitivity that steering requires, which is the asymmetry
we quantify.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Definitions}

\textbf{Readability} is the test AUROC of an $\ell_2$-regularized logistic probe
trained on residual-stream activations at the best layer, evaluated on held-out
\emph{template families} rather than held-out examples, and reported alongside a
shuffled-label control probe so selectivity is explicit
\citep{hewitt2019control}. Evaluating on held-out families rather than held-out
examples is intended to penalize a probe that relies on family-specific surface
cues.

\textbf{Controllability} is the area under the steering dose-response curve up to
a fluency ceiling. We form the steering direction as the difference of class
means, add $\alpha\,\hat v$ at the intervention layer, and sweep $\alpha$ in
units of the layer's residual-stream RMS norm so that a coefficient means the
same thing across layers and models. At each strength we score the behavior with
a fixed judge and measure the perplexity of the output under the unsteered
model. The reported quantity is the mean absolute shift in behavior from baseline,
integrated over usable strengths. We use an integrated measure rather than a
single-point effect size because the latter is sensitive to the choice of
strength.

\textbf{Fluency ceiling.} A strength is treated as unusable if steered-output
perplexity under the unsteered model exceeds twice the unsteered baseline, or if
degenerate repetition appears. The threshold is fixed in advance so that the
largest usable strength is determined by a stated rule rather than chosen after
inspecting the results.

\subsection{Why the two quantities can differ}

A probe at layer $L$ asks a question about the \emph{encoder}: given the first
$L$ layers, is the concept written linearly separably into the residual stream?
Steering at layer $L$ asks about the \emph{decoder}: is the computation of
layers $L{+}1$ onward sensitive to movement along this direction? To first
order, steering by strength $\alpha$ changes behavior by
\[
  \Delta \text{behavior} \;\approx\; \alpha \,\lVert \nabla f(h) \rVert \,
  \cos\!\big(\hat v,\, \nabla f(h)\big),
\]
where $h$ is the layer-$L$ residual stream, $\hat v$ the unit concept direction,
and $f$ the map from $h$ to the behavior score. Controllability is governed by
how much of $\hat v$ lies in the subspace the downstream computation is
sensitive to; readability is governed by a Fisher-style ratio of between-class
to within-class variance along $\hat v$. A concept can have large between-class
separation along a direction to which the downstream computation is largely
insensitive. Readability is a property of how the concept is encoded;
controllability depends on whether later layers are sensitive to that encoding.

\subsection{Terminology}

We distinguish two claims. ``Unsteerable under tested interventions'' refers to
the interventions applied here; ``immovable'' is a stronger claim about the
concept itself. A concept enters the danger zone only if it clears both raw thresholds
(readability AUROC $\geq 0.9$ and controllability area $\leq 0.05$) with
confidence intervals that exclude the thresholds. It is called immovable only if
it then resists a six-intervention gauntlet: single-layer addition, multi-layer
addition, clamping, and directional ablation on the difference-of-means
direction, plus single-layer addition on the probe-weight and RepE
reading-vector directions. Even a confirmed pass is bounded by those six
interventions and does not establish that no intervention exists.

\subsection{The primary geometric hypothesis (H1)}

The most direct restatement of the asymmetry is mechanism M1, read/write
mismatch: a concept is a spectator feature to the extent it is written into a
subspace the output distribution barely depends on. This predicts
controllability rises with \emph{output overlap}, the fraction of the concept
direction lying in the top singular subspace of the unembedding matrix, which is
measurable from weights alone with no extra forward passes. H1 is the
preregistered primary test: partial Spearman of output overlap against
controllability, conditioning on readability, with a cluster bootstrap over
concepts.

\subsection{Judge and reproducibility}

Behavior is scored by a model rather than by humans at scale, a practice with
known position, verbosity and self-preference biases \citep{zheng2023judge}. We
use a fixed logit judge (Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct, read from the Yes/No token
mass), held identical across all four target models so that cross-model
comparison is valid, and validate it against a human rater in the Limitations. A degenerate judge (one score across a whole
sweep) produces a flat dose-response curve, which is the pattern the danger-zone
criterion selects for; such points are detected and excluded. The concept datasets,
probes, steering harness with all robustness variants, and the full gap-map data
are released. Full protocol and hyperparameters are in Appendix~\ref{app:hyper}.

\section{Experimental setup}

\textbf{Models.} Four instruction-tuned families spanning 1.5B to 9B parameters:
Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, and
Gemma-2-9b-it, all run in 4-bit for inference on a single 16GB GPU. Gemma-2 is
loaded with eager attention because its attention soft-cap is ignored by the
fused kernel.

\textbf{Concepts.} Ten concepts spanning easy-to-steer to hard-to-steer:
sentiment (the positive control), certainty, factuality, formality, honesty,
refusal, rudeness, sycophancy \citep{sharma2024sycophancy}, topic (science vs
everyday), and verbosity. Each is built as minimal pairs tagged by template
family.

\textbf{Surface audit.} Before extraction, a leave-one-family-out TF-IDF audit
checks that no concept can be separated on lexical cues alone. Eight concepts
pass (Appendix~\ref{app:concepts}); two (topic and verbosity) are declared
surface-confounded in advance and
reported with that caveat rather than dropped, because domain membership and
length are surface properties by construction.

\textbf{Positive control (P9).} Steering must demonstrably work on a model
before that model's controllability numbers are trusted. A model whose sentiment
control falls below an absolute floor is withheld entirely, because a broken
harness puts every concept in the danger zone and is indistinguishable from a
discovery. All four models pass (sentiment controllability $0.215$, $0.163$,
$0.101$, $0.110$; floor $0.10$).

readability-controllability plane, both axes min-max normalized within model to
remove cross-model scale differences. The Spearman correlation between the two
axes is $0.14$ with a 95\% bootstrap CI of $[-0.24, 0.51]$: on this data,
detection does not predict control. Table~\ref{tab:matrix} gives the underlying

\subsection{Legible concepts that resist the full gauntlet}

Figure~\ref{fig:dose} shows the dissociation on a single model. The danger zone
has two occupants, both \texttt{topic\_science} (on Mistral and
Gemma), each confirmed immovable against all six interventions. It is the least
controllable concept on all four models ($0.019$, $0.024$, $0.011$, $0.007$) and
survived the gauntlet on all four; on Qwen and Llama it sits just outside the
zone only because its readability confidence interval falls below the $0.9$
threshold. This concept is one of the two declared surface-confounded, so its
readability should be interpreted with that caveat. The resistance to steering
is consistent across models, but the concept is not a clean example of a
readable-but-immovable concept.

\texttt{refusal} falls just outside the danger zone on all four models
($0.047$, $0.038$, $0.033$, $0.038$). It is not surface-confounded, it is
safety-relevant, and it survived the gauntlet on two models. It misses the
danger zone on the upper bound of its controllability interval, which exceeds
the $0.05$ threshold on all four models. We report it as a boundary case.

\subsection{The geometric predictor has the wrong sign}
\label{sec:inverted}

H1 predicts that controllability increases with output overlap. The partial
Spearman of output overlap against controllability, conditioning on readability,
is $-0.45$ with a cluster-bootstrap 95\% CI of $[-0.72, -0.04]$. The interval
excludes zero, but on the side opposite to the prediction: the concepts whose
direction is most aligned with the output-effective subspace are the least
steerable.

The effect is driven largely by a single concept. \texttt{topic\_science} has the
highest output overlap of any concept on every model ($0.395$, $0.424$, $0.419$,
$0.536$) and the lowest controllability on every model.

One possible explanation, which we state as a hypothesis rather than a result,
is that output overlap as operationalized here reflects how lexical a concept is
rather than how strongly the downstream computation depends on it. A concept
that maps onto particular output tokens, such as domain vocabulary for
\texttt{topic\_science} or token count for \texttt{verbosity}, projects strongly
into the unembedding subspace. Steering along such a direction may perturb token
selection until fluency degrades, or the concept may be recovered from the
surrounding context, in either case without producing a graded change in
behavior. Concepts such as sentiment have lower output overlap but appear to
engage a downstream computation that responds more smoothly. This account is
consistent with the surface audit, which flags the same high-overlap concepts.

The pattern is not uniform across the low-controllability concepts.
\texttt{refusal} has low output overlap ($0.345$ to $0.382$) and low
controllability, which is more consistent with redundant multi-direction
encoding \citep{joad2026refusal} than with the lexical account. Redundant or
superposed encoding \citep{elhage2022superposition} would allow a concept to be
recovered downstream after one direction is perturbed. Two exploratory geometric features
(effective dimensionality of the class difference, and alignment with
low-variance principal components) are both null after Benjamini-Hochberg
correction \citep{benjamini1995fdr}, and a ridge predictor over all features has leave-one-out $R^2$
near zero. None of the geometric quantities we tested accounts for the gap in
the predicted direction.

\section{Discussion}

Two implications follow for interpretability-based safety. First, probe accuracy
is not evidence that a representation affords control. Across four models the
correlation between readability and controllability is indistinguishable from
zero, and the safety-relevant concepts in our set fall on the
low-controllability side. Arguments that treat a working probe as partial
control are therefore not supported by this data.

Second, the geometric quantity most often assumed to govern steerability has the
wrong sign at this scale. If the lexical account above is correct, projection
into the output-effective subspace measures surface association rather than
downstream causal influence, and a sensitivity-based measure would be more
appropriate. We leave that to future work.

\section{Limitations}

\textbf{The readability axis is saturated.} Thirty-one of forty points sit at
AUROC $1.0$, so the primary $x$-axis has little variance and the reported
correlation is uninformative rather than a null result. The
minimal-pair construction that lets the surface audit pass also makes concepts
trivially separable. We report selectivity (AUROC minus the shuffled-label
control) as a secondary axis with more spread.

\textbf{Judge validation.} We hand-labeled 100 generations stratified across all ten concepts and
compared three raters: a human, the fixed logit judge, and a lexicon scorer.
Agreement must be read within concept rather than pooled, because pooled
agreement over a multi-concept sheet mostly reflects that refusal text scores low
and honesty text scores high, which is trivially true when they answer different
questions; controllability is a within-concept quantity. Within concept, the human
and the logit judge reach Krippendorff's $\alpha$ \citep{krippendorff2019content}
of $0.33$, against $0.18$ for the
lexicon scorer. On the positive control the judge tracks the human closely
($\alpha = 0.76$ for sentiment), so the instrument behaves as intended where
behavior varies. On
\texttt{topic\_science} the judge's spread across sampled generations is $0.02$,
below the human's $0.05$, so the near-zero controllability that places this
concept in the danger zone is unlikely to be a judge artifact.

The exception is \texttt{refusal}. The human scored all ten sampled outputs
identically, while the judge spread them over $0.16$. Because noise in the
behavior score adds apparent movement, the measured controllability for
\texttt{refusal} is more likely an over-estimate than an under-estimate, and its
confidence interval wider than it should be. That interval is the sole reason
\texttt{refusal} misses the danger-zone criterion. We record this as a limitation
rather than argue from it, since determining where \texttt{refusal} lies would
require re-measurement with a better-validated judge. The same pattern appears
on \texttt{honesty}, \texttt{certainty} and \texttt{rudeness};
Appendix~\ref{app:judge} gives the per-concept detail.

\textbf{The strongest confirmed-immovable concept is surface-confounded.} The
readable-but-immovable claim currently rests on a boundary case
(\texttt{refusal}) rather than a slam-dunk, because the two gauntlet-confirmed
points are the surface-confounded \texttt{topic\_science}.

\textbf{One choice of feature basis.} We estimate concept directions by
difference of means, probe weights, and reading vectors, all of which assume
the concept is a direction in the residual stream \citep{park2024linear}.
Sparse autoencoders \citep{cunningham2024sae} and unsupervised methods such as
\citet{burns2023ccs} recover features under different assumptions, and a concept
that is immovable along its difference-of-means direction might be movable
along a feature recovered that way. Our claims are therefore about the
directions these standard estimators produce, not about the concept in the
abstract.

\textbf{Scale and stimuli.} Four instruction-tuned families under 10B parameters,
with templated stimuli. Whether the inversion survives at larger scale or with
naturalistic prompts is open.

\section{Conclusion}

Across four models and ten concepts, readability and controllability are
uncorrelated, and concepts that a linear probe recovers at high accuracy can
resist every steering method we applied. The first-order geometric account of
the gap, under which a concept is steerable to the extent that its direction
projects into the output-effective subspace, has the wrong sign on this data:
the most output-aligned concepts are the least steerable. Probe accuracy is
therefore not evidence that a representation affords control, and projection
into the output subspace is not a reliable predictor of steerability at this
scale.

\subsection*{Ethics statement}

Several concepts in our set are safety-relevant, including refusal, honesty and
sycophancy. These were included because they are the cases in which an
unwarranted assumption about control would have the greatest consequence. The
finding is negative with respect to capability: probe accuracy does not indicate
that a representation affords control, and the safety-relevant concepts were
among the most difficult to move.

We do not release a method for removing safety behavior. The steering
interventions used here are previously published methods, applied at strengths
bounded by a fluency ceiling. The toxicity-adjacent concept in our set is a
courtesy-register proxy rather than genuinely toxic text, so the released
dataset does not contain harmful content.

Hand-labeling was performed by an author rather than by crowd workers. All
models are publicly released instruction-tuned checkpoints used under their
respective licenses.

\subsection*{Reproducibility statement}

All reported numbers are produced by the released code and data. The concept

The full protocol, including the coefficient grid, the preregistered fluency

\section{Concept construction and the surface audit}
\label{app:concepts}

Each concept is constructed as minimal pairs grouped into template families, and
probes are evaluated on held-out families rather than held-out examples. Two
build-time invariants restrict the contrast from being carried by surface
vocabulary: marker words are disjoint across families, and paired markers are
length-matched.

Before extracting activations we run a leave-one-family-out TF-IDF audit that
tries to separate the classes from surface form alone, scored two-sided so a
classifier that ranks a held-out family in reverse order is still treated as
having found a lexical contrast. Eight of ten concepts score exactly $0.500$.
This is the floor of the statistic rather than a strong result: because marker
vocabulary is disjoint by construction, a model trained on five families has no
feature that fires on the sixth. The audit therefore verifies that the
construction succeeded and is not independent evidence that the probe reads
concept meaning. During development it detected duplicated families, markers
shared across families, and a length imbalance that separated families at AUROC
$0.94$ with no marker word transferring.

Two concepts fail the audit and are declared surface-confounded in advance
rather than excused. \texttt{verbosity} is length, which is a surface property by
definition, and scores $1.000$. \texttt{topic\_science} is domain membership,
carried by content vocabulary, and scores $0.833$. Both are reported with the
confound stated. This bears on the main result, since the two gauntlet-confirmed
danger-zone points are \texttt{topic\_science}.

\section{Protocol and hyperparameters}
\label{app:hyper}

\textbf{Models.} Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3,
Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct and Gemma-2-9b-it, all loaded in 4-bit for inference on a
single 16GB accelerator. Gemma-2 is loaded with eager attention because its
attention soft-cap is not applied by the fused kernel.

\textbf{Probes.} $\ell_2$-regularized logistic regression, $C=1.0$, averaged over
three seeds. The layer is selected on a validation family split disjoint from
both train and test, never on test AUROC, with ties broken toward the middle of
the network. Readability is the test AUROC at the selected layer, with a
$1000$-sample bootstrap interval, reported beside a shuffled-label control
probe.

\textbf{Steering.} Directions are difference-of-means unless stated. The
coefficient grid is $\{-3, -2, -1, -0.5, 0, 0.5, 1, 2, 3\}$ in units of the
residual-stream RMS norm at the intervention layer, so a coefficient means the
same thing across layers and models. The intervention spans a band of four
layers centered on the probe layer. A strength is unusable if steered-output
perplexity under the unsteered model exceeds twice the unsteered baseline, or
if degenerate repetition appears. The threshold is fixed in advance.

\textbf{Gauntlet.} Single-layer addition, multi-layer addition, clamping and
directional ablation on the difference-of-means direction, plus single-layer
addition on the probe-weight and RepE reading-vector directions. The gauntlet is
run only on concepts that the default intervention failed to move; a concept
that never entered the gauntlet is recorded as such rather than as having failed
it.

\textbf{Thresholds.} A point enters the danger zone at raw readability
$\geq 0.9$ and raw controllability $\leq 0.05$, in both cases with the bootstrap
interval excluding the threshold. The positive control floor is a
dose-response area of $0.10$.

\textbf{Judge.} A single fixed judge, Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct in fp16, scored
from the Yes/No token mass in a single forward pass rather than by parsing
generated text. The judge is held identical across all four target models, since
danger-zone membership is decided on raw controllability and a judge that varied
with the condition would make those values incomparable. Each result record
stores the judge identity and a flag indicating whether the model under study
scored its own outputs.

\textbf{Compute.} The full study is roughly ten accelerator-hours on two T4s,
dominated by the steering sweeps. The judge validation adds about ninety
seconds.
