\begin{abstract}
Activation steering is usually evaluated by adding a concept direction to the
residual stream and reporting how much behavior changes. We show that this
measurement, in its standard absolute-deviation form, does not distinguish
steering a model in the intended direction from merely perturbing it, and that a
substantive conclusion drawn from it does not survive the distinction. For ten
concepts across three instruction-tuned models we sweep a steering coefficient to
a preregistered fluency ceiling and compute two quantities from the same curves:
the usual area under the absolute behavioral deviation, and its signed analogue,
which credits an intervention only when positive coefficients raise the target
behavior and negative coefficients lower it. The two are uncorrelated (Spearman
$-0.01$ over 30 concept-model points). Sixteen of the thirty points have a
negative signed area, and the median point attributes $39$\% of its measured
effect to directional movement. Response direction is close to random across
models: two of ten concepts are same-signed on all three, against $2.5$ expected
by chance. We then show what this costs a substantive result. We preregistered
the hypothesis that a direction is steerable to the extent it projects into the
model's output-effective (unembedding) subspace. Under the absolute metric the
estimate is inverted (partial Spearman $-0.45$, conditioning on readability), and
the inversion is not an artifact of the fluency ceiling: output overlap is
unrelated to where the ceiling falls, 27 of 30 sweeps reach the end of the
coefficient grid unbroken, and conditioning on the ceiling leaves the estimate
unchanged. Leave-one-concept-out keeps it negative in every refit. Under the
signed metric the same test returns $+0.14$. The geometric result is therefore a
property of how controllability was measured rather than of the representations.
Held-out probe AUROC is $1.00$ at 24 of 30 points, so the near-zero
readability-controllability correlation is uninformative rather than a null, and
the supportable claim is the weaker one: high linear readability does not
guarantee an effective control handle. A fourth model is withheld throughout
because it fails the preregistered positive control under the directional metric.
We release the stimuli, probes, steering harness, and per-coefficient curves.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Activation steering is evaluated by intervening on a model and measuring the
change in its behavior. In the common form of this measurement a concept
direction is estimated from contrastive activations, added to the residual
stream at a range of strengths, and the resulting behavioral shift is summarized
as a magnitude: how far the model moved. That summary is silent on a question it
appears to answer, namely whether the model moved the way the direction was
supposed to move it.

The distinction matters because the two come apart. An intervention that
displaces activations along an arbitrary direction will change what a model says,
and a metric that records the size of that change will score it. Only a metric
that tracks the sign of the change can separate control from disturbance. We
compute both from the same dose-response curves and find them unrelated: over 30
concept-model points the absolute and signed summaries correlate at $-0.01$, and
the median point owes under $40$\% of its measured effect to movement in the
intended direction.

This is a claim about an instrument, so we test what it does to a result obtained
with that instrument. We had preregistered a geometric account of when steering
should work, in which a direction is controllable to the extent it projects into
the subspace that drives the model's output distribution. Measured with the
absolute metric the relationship is inverted, and the inversion is robust to the
checks that would ordinarily be asked of it: it is not produced by the fluency
ceiling truncating the coefficient sweep, and it does not depend on any single
concept. Measured with the signed metric it disappears. We report the whole
sequence because the robustness checks are what make the point: a result can pass
the diagnostics the field asks for and still be an artifact of the summary
statistic underneath it.

The study was built to ask a different question, whether concepts a probe can
detect are concepts steering can control, and we keep that framing because it is
how the data were collected and preregistered. \emph{Readability} is the held-out
AUROC of a linear probe; \emph{controllability} is the dose-response area up to
the fluency ceiling. That axis carries its own lesson: probe accuracy saturates
at $1.00$ on most points, so the flat relationship we observe is uninformative
rather than a null, and only the weaker claim is supportable.

Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item A directional diagnostic for steering evaluations, and the finding that
  the standard absolute-deviation summary is orthogonal to it across ten concepts
  and three models (Section~\ref{sec:directional}). Within-concept response
  direction is no more consistent across models than chance.
  \item A worked demonstration that a preregistered geometric result survives a
  ceiling control and leave-one-concept-out but not a change of metric
  (Section~\ref{sec:inverted}), with the controls reported in full.
  \item A robustness protocol that separates ``unsteerable under the interventions
  we tried'' from ``immovable.'' A concept is only called immovable if it
  resists all six of single- and multi-layer activation addition, clamping,
  directional ablation, and steering on both the probe-weight and RepE
  reading-vector directions (Section~\ref{sec:method}).
  \item An account of what a saturated readability axis can and cannot establish
  (Section~\ref{sec:results}), and a positive control applied strictly enough to
  withhold a model.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Reading concepts out of activations}

Linear probes on intermediate activations were introduced as a diagnostic for
what a network has computed by a given depth \citep{alain2016probes}, and the
practice is now standard in language-model interpretability. What a probe
licenses has been contested for almost as long. \citet{hewitt2019control} show
that probe accuracy alone is uninterpretable, because a sufficiently expressive
probe can fit an arbitrary control task, and propose reporting selectivity, the
gap between task and control accuracy; we adopt that recommendation and report
a shuffled-label control for every concept. \citet{belinkov2022probing}
surveys the resulting methodological literature and its open problems.

The linear representation hypothesis \citep{park2024linear} supplies the
justification for treating a concept as a direction at all, and underwrites
difference-of-means estimation. It is a claim about how concepts are encoded.
Nothing in it connects the between-class separation a probe exploits to the
downstream causal sensitivity that steering requires, which is precisely the
asymmetry we quantify. Unsupervised methods recover candidate directions
without labelled contrasts, either from logical consistency
\citep{burns2023ccs} or by dictionary learning over activations
\citep{cunningham2024sae}; we use supervised contrasts throughout, and return to
that choice in the Limitations.

\subsection{From reading to intervening}

The closest methodological ancestor of this paper is amnesic probing
\citep{elazar2021amnesic}, which argues that probing accuracy does not establish
that a model uses the probed property, and tests use by removing the property
from the representation and measuring the behavioral consequence. The
underlying removal operation has its own literature: iterative nullspace
projection \citep{ravfogel2020inlp} removes linearly decodable information by
repeated projection, and LEACE \citep{belrose2023leace} does so in closed form
while minimally perturbing the representation. Directional ablation, one of the
six interventions in our robustness battery, is the single-direction case of
this operation.

Our question is the mirror image. Amnesic probing removes information and asks
whether behavior degrades; we add signal along a direction and ask whether
behavior moves, sweeping strength rather than testing a single removal. The two
can come apart: a concept the model genuinely uses may still be immovable if the
downstream computation is insensitive to displacement along the particular
direction a probe or a difference of means recovers.

More broadly, editing activations to test what a component does is standard
practice, from causal tracing and rank-one weight editing
\citep{meng2022rome} onward. The activation-patching methodology literature
documents how strongly the reported conclusion depends on the metric and the
corruption chosen \citep{zhang2023patching}, which is one reason we fix the
dose-response metric and the fluency ceiling in advance rather than selecting
them after seeing results. Our interventions belong to this family but are
applied to a fixed concept direction rather than to a located component.

\subsection{Steering methods and their known limits}

Difference-of-means and contrastive directions are the standard tools for
moving concepts. Activation addition \citep{turner2023actadd} adds a direction
estimated from a contrast pair into the residual stream at inference time; its
contrastive-pairs refinement \citep{panickssery2023caa} estimates the direction
from many pairs; inference-time intervention \citep{li2023iti} selects attention
heads by probe accuracy and shifts along the probe direction; and
representation engineering \citep{zou2023repe} generalizes reading and
controlling representations into a single framework. We use these as our
interventions rather than compare against them: the paper measures how far a
concept moves under standard methods, not which method moves it furthest.

Negative results about steering already exist. \citet{tan2024steering} report
that steerability varies substantially across inputs within a concept, and that
steering vectors can be brittle to reasonable prompt changes, so a vector that
works on average may fail on particular inputs. That variation is
within-concept and across inputs. Ours is across concepts, and is paired with a
detection measurement on the same concepts, which is what makes a dissociation
rather than a reliability problem visible. The two findings compound: a method
that is unreliable across inputs and, separately, inert for some concepts is
weaker evidence for control than either result alone suggests.

\subsection{Directions that do carry causal weight}

The dissociation we report is not a claim that concept directions never
control behavior, and the counterexamples matter for calibrating it.
\citet{marks2023truth} find that difference-in-mean probes on true/false
statements recover directions that are more causally implicated in model
outputs than those from other probing methods, which is the same estimator we
use finding a direction that does move behavior. \citet{todd2024function} show
that a compact vector extracted from in-context demonstrations triggers
execution of the demonstrated task in contexts that do not resemble the ones it
was collected from. Both are existence proofs that a direction read out of
activations can be a control handle. Our result is that whether it is cannot be
assumed from detection accuracy, and varies by concept in a way that the
obvious geometric quantity does not predict.

Relatedly, \citet{bolukbasi2021illusion} document an interpretability illusion
in which directions appear to encode a simple concept while in fact encoding
something else, traced partly to the narrowness of the corpora used to probe
them. Their illusion concerns what a direction means; ours concerns what a
direction does. Both are reasons to treat a high probe score as the start of an
argument rather than its conclusion.

\subsection{Refusal, and the one prior detection-versus-control result}

Refusal is the most studied single concept in this literature.
\citet{arditi2024refusal} show that refusal is mediated by a direction whose
ablation prevents refusal across many chat models, which is the strongest
existing case that a concept direction is a control handle.
\citet{joad2026refusal} complicate this: refusal spans eleven categories on
geometrically distinct directions that nonetheless behave as a shared
one-dimensional control. That a concept can be carried redundantly is one
candidate explanation for resistance to intervention on any single direction,
and is why we read refusal's behavior in our data as consistent with redundant
encoding rather than with the lexical account we offer elsewhere.

\citet{galeone2026perfect} report the one prior result measuring detection
against control directly: on a hallucination case, fake entities are detected
with perfect linear separability while the detection direction sits nearly
orthogonal to the direction that controls the behavior, across four models and
surviving instruction tuning. Their measurement is geometric, an angle between
two directions on one behavior. Ours is behavioral and comparative, sweeping an
intervention across ten concepts and three models. Our contribution is not that
gap but the finding that the standard way of measuring the second quantity does
not measure what it is taken to.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Definitions}

\textbf{Readability} is the test AUROC of an $\ell_2$-regularized logistic probe
trained on residual-stream activations at the best layer, evaluated on held-out
\emph{template families} rather than held-out examples, and reported alongside a
shuffled-label control probe so selectivity is explicit
\citep{hewitt2019control}. Evaluating on held-out families rather than held-out
examples is intended to penalize a probe that relies on family-specific surface
cues.

\textbf{Controllability} is the area under the steering dose-response curve up to
a fluency ceiling. We form the steering direction as the difference of class
means, add $\alpha\,\hat v$ at the intervention layer, and sweep $\alpha$ in
units of the layer's residual-stream RMS norm so that a coefficient means the
same thing across layers and models. At each strength we score the behavior with
a fixed judge and measure the perplexity of the output under the unsteered
model. The reported quantity is the mean absolute shift in behavior from baseline,
integrated over usable strengths. We use an integrated measure rather than a
single-point effect size because the latter is sensitive to the choice of
strength.

\textbf{Directional controllability} is the same integral without the absolute
value, integrating $\mathrm{sign}(\alpha)\,(b(\alpha) - b_0)$ over the same usable
strengths, where $b(\alpha)$ is judged behavior and $b_0$ the unsteered baseline.
It is positive only when positive coefficients raise the target behavior and
negative coefficients lower it, so it credits an intervention for moving the
model the way the direction was built to move it and penalizes it for moving the
model the other way. Absolute controllability remains the preregistered primary
measure and every preregistered threshold is applied to it; the directional
quantity is reported alongside it as a diagnostic, and Section~\ref{sec:directional}
is what happens when the two disagree.

\textbf{Fluency ceiling.} A strength is treated as unusable if steered-output
perplexity under the unsteered model exceeds twice the unsteered baseline, or if
degenerate repetition appears. The threshold is fixed in advance so that the
largest usable strength is determined by a stated rule rather than chosen after
inspecting the results.

\subsection{Why the two quantities can differ}

A probe at layer $L$ asks a question about the \emph{encoder}: given the first
$L$ layers, is the concept written linearly separably into the residual stream?
Steering at layer $L$ asks about the \emph{decoder}: is the computation of
layers $L{+}1$ onward sensitive to movement along this direction? To first
order, steering by strength $\alpha$ changes behavior by
\[
  \Delta \text{behavior} \;\approx\; \alpha \,\lVert \nabla f(h) \rVert \,
  \cos\!\big(\hat v,\, \nabla f(h)\big),
\]
where $h$ is the layer-$L$ residual stream, $\hat v$ the unit concept direction,
and $f$ the map from $h$ to the behavior score. Controllability is governed by
how much of $\hat v$ lies in the subspace the downstream computation is
sensitive to; readability is governed by a Fisher-style ratio of between-class
to within-class variance along $\hat v$. A concept can have large between-class
separation along a direction to which the downstream computation is largely
insensitive. Readability is a property of how the concept is encoded;
controllability depends on whether later layers are sensitive to that encoding.

\subsection{Terminology}

We distinguish two claims. ``Unsteerable under tested interventions'' refers to
the interventions applied here; ``immovable'' is a stronger claim about the
concept itself. A concept enters the danger zone only if it clears both raw thresholds
(readability AUROC $\geq 0.9$ and controllability area $\leq 0.05$) with
confidence intervals that exclude the thresholds. It is called immovable only if
it then resists a six-intervention gauntlet: single-layer addition, multi-layer
addition, clamping, and directional ablation on the difference-of-means
direction, plus single-layer addition on the probe-weight and RepE
reading-vector directions. Appendix~\ref{app:gauntlet} reports what each of the
six achieved on every point that entered the battery. Even a confirmed pass is
bounded by those six interventions and does not establish that no intervention
exists.

\subsection{The primary geometric hypothesis (H1)}

The most direct restatement of the asymmetry is mechanism M1, read/write
mismatch: a concept is a spectator feature to the extent it is written into a
subspace the output distribution barely depends on. This predicts
controllability rises with \emph{output overlap}, the fraction of the concept
direction lying in the top-$k$ right singular subspace of the unembedding matrix,
which is measurable from weights alone with no extra forward passes. We fix
$k=512$ in advance for every model (Appendix~\ref{app:hyper}) and did not sweep
it, so the test is conditional on that choice. H1 is the
preregistered primary test: partial Spearman of output overlap against
controllability, conditioning on readability, with a cluster bootstrap over
concepts \citep{cameron2008bootstrap}, since the 30 retained points are three
observations of each of ten concepts and are not independent.

\subsection{Judge and reproducibility}

Behavior is scored by a model rather than by humans at scale, a practice with
known position, verbosity and self-preference biases \citep{zheng2023judge}. We
use a fixed logit judge (Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct, read from the Yes/No token
mass), held identical across every target model so that cross-model
comparison is valid, and validate it against a human rater in the Limitations. A degenerate judge (one score across a whole
sweep) produces a flat dose-response curve, which is the pattern the danger-zone
criterion selects for; such points are detected and excluded. The concept datasets,
probes, steering harness with all robustness variants, and the full gap-map data
are released. Full protocol and hyperparameters are in Appendix~\ref{app:hyper}.

\section{Experimental setup}

\textbf{Models.} Four instruction-tuned families spanning 7B to 9B parameters were
run: Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, and
Gemma-2-9b-it, all in 4-bit for inference on a single 16GB GPU. Gemma-2 is
loaded with eager attention because its attention soft-cap is ignored by the
fused kernel.

Gemma-2-9b-it is withheld under the positive control described below, so all
results are the remaining three models, 30 concept-model points. Its numbers
appear only in Appendix~\ref{app:full}.

\textbf{Concepts.} Ten concepts spanning easy-to-steer to hard-to-steer:
sentiment (the positive control), certainty, factuality, formality, honesty,
refusal, rudeness, sycophancy \citep{sharma2024sycophancy}, topic (science vs
everyday), and verbosity. Each is built as minimal pairs tagged by template
family.

\textbf{Surface audit.} Before extraction, a leave-one-family-out TF-IDF audit
checks that no concept can be separated on lexical cues alone. Eight concepts
pass (Appendix~\ref{app:concepts}); two (topic and verbosity) are declared
surface-confounded in advance and
reported with that caveat rather than dropped (Appendix~\ref{app:stimuli} gives
example stimuli for every concept), because domain membership and
length are surface properties by construction.

\textbf{Positive control (P9).} Steering must demonstrably work on a model
before that model's controllability numbers are trusted. A model whose sentiment
control falls below an absolute floor is withheld entirely, because a broken
harness puts every concept in the danger zone and is indistinguishable from a
discovery.

On the absolute metric all four models clear the floor (sentiment controllability
$0.215$ Qwen, $0.163$ Mistral, $0.101$ Llama, $0.110$ Gemma; floor $0.10$). The
control is less reassuring read directionally. Sentiment dose-response
monotonicity is $+0.79$, $+0.98$ and $+0.50$ on the three retained models and
$-0.68$ on Gemma, whose signed area is $-0.039$: on that model the harness moved
sentiment the wrong way while still registering a passing absolute score. Because
Section~\ref{sec:directional} makes the directional reading the one the paper
relies on, Gemma is withheld under the preregistered rule and is excluded from
every statistic in the main text.

We note two things against ourselves here. Llama clears the floor by $0.001$, and
if interval exclusion were applied to the floor as it is to the danger-zone
thresholds, Llama and Mistral would be withheld too and nothing would remain. And
the fact that a model can fail directionally while passing on the absolute metric
is the same observation the paper is about, arriving in the control rather than
in the results.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{What the saturated readability axis can support}

Figure~\ref{fig:gapmap} is the gap map: every retained concept-model pair in the
readability-controllability plane, both axes min-max normalized within model to
remove cross-model scale differences. The Spearman correlation between the two
axes is $0.12$ with a 95\% bootstrap CI of $[-0.28, 0.58]$.

That number should not be read as a null. Readability is at $1.00$ for 24 of the
30 points and never falls below $0.90$ except on three points, two of them
\texttt{sycophancy} probes that failed outright at $0.57$. Excluding the three
points whose probe did not reach $0.9$ moves the correlation from $+0.14$ to
$+0.26$ ($n=27$, $p=0.20$), which does not change the reading: neither value is
distinguishable from zero and neither can be, for the reason that follows. A predictor with almost
no variance cannot be shown to be unrelated to anything, and a correlation
computed against it is uninformative rather than evidence of independence. The
claim the data support is the conditional one: among concepts a probe recovers
essentially perfectly, controllability still ranges from $0.011$ to $0.215$, a
factor of twenty. High linear readability does not guarantee a control handle. It
is the converse, that low readability implies low controllability, that this
design cannot address, because it contains almost no unreadable concepts.

Appendix~\ref{app:saturation} reports the same correlation against probe
selectivity, which does vary, and finds nothing there either.
Table~\ref{tab:matrix} gives the underlying
readability and controllability by concept and model. Appendix~\ref{app:full}
adds confidence intervals, selectivity, the shuffled-label control probe, the
selected layer and the robustness-battery outcome for every point.

\subsection{Legible concepts that resist the full gauntlet}

Figure~\ref{fig:dose} shows the dissociation on a single model. The danger zone
has two occupants, both \texttt{topic\_science} (on Mistral and
Gemma), each confirmed immovable against all six interventions. It is the least
controllable concept on all three retained models ($0.019$, $0.024$, $0.011$) and
survived the gauntlet on all three; on Qwen and Llama it sits just outside the
zone only because its readability confidence interval falls below the $0.9$
threshold. This concept is one of the two declared surface-confounded, so its
readability should be interpreted with that caveat. The resistance to steering
is consistent across models, but the concept is not a clean example of a
readable-but-immovable concept.

\texttt{refusal} falls just outside the danger zone on all three retained models
($0.047$, $0.038$, $0.033$, $0.038$). It is not surface-confounded, it is
safety-relevant, and it survived the gauntlet on two models. It misses the
danger zone on the upper bound of its controllability interval, which exceeds
the $0.05$ threshold on all three retained models. We report it as a boundary case.


\subsection{The controllability metric does not measure direction}
\label{sec:directional}

Every number above summarizes a dose-response curve by the area under the
absolute deviation from baseline. That summary counts any departure from
baseline as control. It is worth asking how much of what it counts is movement
in the direction the concept direction was built to produce.

Recomputing the same integral without the absolute value gives the directional
controllability of Section~\ref{sec:method}. The two are uncorrelated across the
30 retained points (Spearman $-0.01$). Sixteen of the thirty have a negative
directional area, meaning steering moved behavior against the direction
estimated from the concept's own minimal pairs, and the median point owes $39\%$
of its measured effect to directional movement. Figure~\ref{fig:directional} and
Table~\ref{tab:directional} give the comparison; no point reaches the line on
which the two agree.

The weaker reading of this is that the absolute metric overstates control. The
stronger reading is available from the same table. Within-concept response
direction is close to random across models: only two of ten concepts have the
same sign of dose-response monotonicity on all three models, against $2.5$
expected if each model's sign were an independent coin flip, and the median
monotonicity is $-0.04$. If steering produced a consistent but small directional
effect, the signs would agree and the magnitudes would be small. They do not
agree. For most concepts in this set the intervention is not producing a
reliable directional effect at all.

Two alternative explanations are worth ruling out. The first is a sign
convention error, which would invert a concept's direction and make our
diagnostic score it backwards. \texttt{certainty} is the candidate, with a
negative response on every model. But a systematic polarity error would show up
as many concepts agreeing across models, and the count of same-signed concepts
is what chance predicts. The second is judge noise. If the wrong-way movement
were an artifact of an unreliable judge it should concentrate in the concepts
where our human validation shows the least agreement. It does not: wrong-way
points appear at the same rate in the four concepts with near-zero human
variance as in the six without (Appendix~\ref{app:judge}).

This does not retract the absolute measurements, which are what they are. It
bounds what may be concluded from them, and Section~\ref{sec:inverted} is the
worked case.

\subsection{A geometric result that depends on the metric}
\label{sec:inverted}

H1 predicts that controllability increases with output overlap. Measured with the
absolute metric the partial Spearman of output overlap against controllability,
conditioning on readability, is $-0.45$ with a cluster-bootstrap 95\% CI of
$[-0.75, 0.04]$: the estimate points the opposite way to the prediction, and the
interval contains zero. Figure~\ref{fig:inversion} plots the relationship. On the
full four-model sample before the positive control withheld Gemma the same
interval was $[-0.72, -0.04]$ and did exclude zero, which is worth stating
plainly: the significance of this result did not survive applying our own
preregistered control.

We report the rest of the analysis because the sign is stable and because what
removes it is instructive. Two checks a reader would reasonably demand both pass.

\textbf{The fluency ceiling does not produce it.} A lexical direction might break
fluency at low strength, truncating the usable coefficient range and shrinking an
integrated area mechanically, which would create the observed relationship with
no claim about downstream sensitivity. It does not happen here. Output overlap is
unrelated to where the ceiling falls (Spearman $-0.10$, $p=0.61$), 27 of the 30
sweeps reach the end of the coefficient grid without breaking, and conditioning
the primary test on the ceiling and on the number of usable grid points leaves
the estimate unchanged (Table~\ref{tab:ceiling}; Appendix~\ref{app:controls}
gives both controls in full). \texttt{topic\_science}, the
concept that most invites this objection, completes all nine coefficients on two
of the three retained models with a flat curve rather than a truncated one
(Figure~\ref{fig:ceiling}).

\textbf{No single concept produces it.} \texttt{topic\_science} has the highest
output overlap on every model and the lowest controllability on every model, so
it is the natural suspect. Leave-one-concept-out leaves the estimate negative in
all ten refits, ranging from $-0.22$ to $-0.55$ (Table~\ref{tab:loco},
Figure~\ref{fig:loco}). Dropping \texttt{topic\_science} gives the weakest of
them, $-0.22$, so it does contribute disproportionately, but the sign does not
depend on it.

\textbf{The metric does produce it.} Recomputing the identical test on directional
controllability gives $+0.14$ with a CI of $[-0.27, 0.44]$. The sign reverses and
the relationship disappears. Since Section~\ref{sec:directional} shows the
absolute metric is uncorrelated with the directional one, the honest reading is
that H1 was tested against a quantity that does not track directional control,
and the inversion is a property of that quantity rather than of the
representations. A result can survive a ceiling control and a
leave-one-concept-out and still be an artifact of its own summary statistic.

If one wanted to retain the absolute-metric reading, the available explanation,
which we state as a hypothesis rather than a result,
is that output overlap as operationalized here reflects how lexical a concept is
rather than how strongly the downstream computation depends on it. A concept
that maps onto particular output tokens, such as domain vocabulary for
\texttt{topic\_science} or token count for \texttt{verbosity}, projects strongly
into the unembedding subspace. Steering along such a direction may perturb token
selection until fluency degrades, or the concept may be recovered from the
surrounding context, in either case without producing a graded change in
behavior. Concepts such as sentiment have lower output overlap but appear to
engage a downstream computation that responds more smoothly. This account is
consistent with the surface audit, which flags the same high-overlap concepts.

The pattern is not uniform across the low-controllability concepts.
\texttt{refusal} has low output overlap ($0.345$ to $0.382$) and low
controllability, which is more consistent with redundant multi-direction
encoding \citep{joad2026refusal} than with the lexical account. Redundant or
superposed encoding \citep{elhage2022superposition} would allow a concept to be
recovered downstream after one direction is perturbed. Two exploratory geometric features
(effective dimensionality of the class difference, and alignment with
low-variance principal components) are both null after Benjamini-Hochberg
correction \citep{benjamini1995fdr}, and a ridge predictor over all features has leave-one-out $R^2$
near zero. None of the geometric quantities we tested accounts for the gap in
the predicted direction.

\section{Discussion}

The main implication is for how steering is evaluated. A dose-response summarized
by absolute deviation answers the question of whether an intervention perturbed
the model, not whether it steered it, and on this data the two answers are
unrelated. Any conclusion drawn from such a summary inherits that ambiguity,
including our own preregistered geometric hypothesis, which reverses sign when
the metric is made directional. We recommend that steering evaluations report a
signed dose-response alongside any absolute measure, and treat the two agreeing
as a precondition for interpreting the magnitude. The signed version costs
nothing: it is the same curves and the same judge calls.

A second implication is narrower and concerns what probes license. Among concepts
this probe recovers essentially perfectly, controllability still varies by a
factor of twenty, so a working probe is not evidence of a control handle. The
converse claim, that unreadable concepts are uncontrollable, is not something
this design can address, because it contains almost no unreadable concepts. We
state the asymmetry because the stronger and more quotable version, that
detection does not predict control, would require variance on an axis that has
none here.

Neither implication says that concept directions never control behavior. The
existence proofs cited in Section~\ref{sec:related} stand. What this data
questions is the inference from a measured effect size to a claim of control,
when the measurement does not separate the two.

\section{Limitations}

\textbf{The primary metric is the object of study.} Absolute controllability is
the preregistered measure and every preregistered threshold is applied to it, so
the danger zone, the gauntlet verdicts and the geometric test are all computed on
a quantity Section~\ref{sec:directional} shows does not track direction. We
report them because they were preregistered and because the disagreement between
the two metrics is the result, not because the absolute numbers should be read
as measures of control.

\textbf{Ten concepts, three models.} The cluster bootstrap resamples concepts,
and ten is few enough that inference over-rejects relative to its nominal size
\citep{cameron2008bootstrap}, so the intervals are indicative and the sign
carries more weight than the endpoints. Withholding a model cost a quarter of
the sample and moved the primary interval across zero. Adding concepts, not
models, is what would tighten this.

\textbf{The readability axis is saturated.} Twenty-four of thirty points sit at
AUROC $1.0$, because the minimal-pair construction that lets the surface audit
pass also makes concepts trivially separable. The axis therefore supports only
the conditional claim of Section~\ref{sec:results}.
Appendix~\ref{app:selectivity} reports selectivity, which has more spread, as a
secondary axis.

\textbf{One annotator, and a judge that disagrees with them.} Within-concept
agreement between the human and the logit judge is Krippendorff's $\alpha$
\citep{krippendorff2019content} of $0.33$, against $0.18$ for a lexicon scorer,
and $0.76$ on the sentiment control. But on \texttt{refusal} the human scored
all ten sampled outputs identically while the judge spread them over $0.16$, and
the same pattern holds for \texttt{honesty}, \texttt{certainty} and
\texttt{rudeness}. Because noise in the behavior score adds apparent movement,
controllability on those four concepts is more likely an over-estimate than an
under-estimate. The labels come from one annotator who was blind to condition
but not to the hypothesis. A second annotator and a judge of comparable scale to
the target models are the two things this study most needs and does not have;
Appendix~\ref{app:limits} gives the detail.

\textbf{Nothing clean occupies the danger zone.} The only gauntlet-confirmed
occupant is \texttt{topic\_science}, declared surface-confounded in advance. The
strongest non-confounded candidate, \texttt{refusal}, misses the criterion on an
interval that the judge noise above inflates.

\textbf{One feature basis, one scale.} Directions come from difference of means,
probe weights and reading vectors, all assuming the concept is a direction in
the residual stream \citep{park2024linear}. Sparse autoencoders
\citep{cunningham2024sae} or unsupervised methods \citep{burns2023ccs} could
recover a movable feature where these do not, so the claims are about the
directions standard estimators produce. All models are instruction-tuned and
under 10B parameters, with templated stimuli.

\section{Conclusion}

Steering is usually scored by how far a model moves. Across three models and ten
concepts we find that this score is uncorrelated with whether the model moved in
the intended direction, that half the measured effects point the wrong way, and
that within-concept response direction agrees across models no more often than
chance. A preregistered geometric hypothesis tested against the usual score comes
out inverted, survives a fluency-ceiling control and leave-one-concept-out, and
then disappears when the score is made directional. Among concepts our probe
recovers at AUROC $1.0$, controllability still varies twentyfold, so a working
probe is not evidence of a control handle; the converse is not something this
design can test. The practical recommendation is small and cheap: report a signed
dose-response next to any absolute one, and treat their agreement as a condition
for interpreting the magnitude.

\section{Concept construction and the surface audit}
\label{app:concepts}

Each concept is constructed as minimal pairs grouped into template families, and
probes are evaluated on held-out families rather than held-out examples. Two
build-time invariants restrict the contrast from being carried by surface
vocabulary: marker words are disjoint across families, and paired markers are
length-matched.

Before extracting activations we run a leave-one-family-out TF-IDF audit that
tries to separate the classes from surface form alone, scored two-sided so a
classifier that ranks a held-out family in reverse order is still treated as
having found a lexical contrast. Eight of ten concepts score exactly $0.500$.
This is the floor of the statistic rather than a strong result: because marker
vocabulary is disjoint by construction, a model trained on five families has no
feature that fires on the sixth. The audit therefore verifies that the
construction succeeded and is not independent evidence that the probe reads
concept meaning. During development it detected duplicated families, markers
shared across families, and a length imbalance that separated families at AUROC
$0.94$ with no marker word transferring.

Two concepts fail the audit and are declared surface-confounded in advance
rather than excused. \texttt{verbosity} is length, which is a surface property by
definition, and scores $1.000$. \texttt{topic\_science} is domain membership,
carried by content vocabulary, and scores $0.833$. Both are reported with the
confound stated. This bears on the main result, since the two gauntlet-confirmed
danger-zone points are \texttt{topic\_science}.

\section{Protocol and hyperparameters}
\label{app:hyper}

\textbf{Models.} Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3,
Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct and Gemma-2-9b-it, all loaded in 4-bit for inference on a
single 16GB accelerator. Gemma-2 is loaded with eager attention because its
attention soft-cap is not applied by the fused kernel.

\textbf{Probes.} $\ell_2$-regularized logistic regression, $C=1.0$, averaged over
three seeds. The layer is selected on a validation family split disjoint from
both train and test, never on test AUROC, with ties broken toward the middle of
the network. Readability is the test AUROC at the selected layer, with a
$1000$-sample bootstrap interval, reported beside a shuffled-label control
probe.

\textbf{Steering.} Directions are difference-of-means unless stated. The
coefficient grid is $\{-3, -2, -1, -0.5, 0, 0.5, 1, 2, 3\}$ in units of the
residual-stream RMS norm at the intervention layer, so a coefficient means the
same thing across layers and models. The intervention spans a band of four
layers centered on the probe layer, clipped at the ends of the network; two of
the 30 retained points are clipped this way (\texttt{formality} selects layer 0
and \texttt{sycophancy} layer 2 on one model each), so their band is narrower
than a mid-network point's and not strictly comparable to it.

\textbf{What steering is measured on.} The dose-response is evaluated on each
concept's \texttt{eval\_prompts}, a set of open-ended generation prompts that is
disjoint by construction from the minimal pairs the direction is estimated from:
the direction comes from the pairs, the effect is measured on the prompts, and no
text appears in both. The steering result is therefore not evaluated on the
templates it was fitted to. The set is small, six prompts per concept, which
bounds the resolution of every controllability number in the paper and is the
cheapest thing to enlarge in a rerun.

\textbf{Output overlap.} We take the top $k=512$ right singular vectors of the
unembedding matrix by randomized SVD, computed once per model and cached, and
report the Euclidean norm of the unit-normalized concept direction's projection
onto that subspace, a quantity in $[0,1]$. The value $512$ was fixed in advance
as a tractable stand-in for the full vocabulary, not tuned: a full decomposition
of a $152{,}000 \times 3584$ head per concept was out of budget. We report the
primary test at this $k$ only, and its sensitivity to $k$ is untested, which is a
real gap given that the test's sign is the paper's claim about it. Note that the
\texttt{n\_directions} feature in the released results is a different quantity,
the effective dimensionality of the per-family direction set, and is not $k$. A strength is unusable if steered-output
perplexity under the unsteered model exceeds twice the unsteered baseline, or
if degenerate repetition appears. The threshold is fixed in advance.

\textbf{Gauntlet.} Single-layer addition, multi-layer addition, clamping and
directional ablation on the difference-of-means direction, plus single-layer
addition on the probe-weight and RepE reading-vector directions. The gauntlet is
run only on concepts that the default intervention failed to move; a concept
that never entered the gauntlet is recorded as such rather than as having failed
it.

\textbf{Thresholds.} A point enters the danger zone at raw readability
$\geq 0.9$ and raw controllability $\leq 0.05$, in both cases with the bootstrap
interval excluding the threshold. The positive control floor is a
dose-response area of $0.10$.

\textbf{Judge.} A single fixed judge, Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct in fp16, scored
from the Yes/No token mass in a single forward pass rather than by parsing
generated text. The judge is held identical across all four target models, since
danger-zone membership is decided on raw controllability and a judge that varied
with the condition would make those values incomparable. Each result record
stores the judge identity and a flag indicating whether the model under study
scored its own outputs.

\textbf{Compute.} The full study is roughly ten accelerator-hours on two T4s,
dominated by the steering sweeps. The judge validation adds about ninety
seconds.

\section{The saturated axis, and selectivity as an alternative}
\label{app:selectivity}
\label{app:saturation}

Readability is the preregistered $x$-axis and it is saturated: 24 of 30 points
sit at AUROC $1.0$, so the axis carries almost no variance and the headline
correlation is uninformative rather than a clean null. The cause is the
concept construction itself. Minimal pairs whose marker vocabulary is disjoint
across template families are what makes the surface audit pass, and they also
make every concept trivially separable in activation space.

Selectivity, the probe AUROC minus its shuffled-label control, spreads the same
points across roughly half the unit interval (Figure~\ref{fig:selectivity}).
We report it as a secondary axis rather than substituting it for the
preregistered one. It is not a drop-in replacement: selectivity mixes how
readable a concept is with how much structure the shuffled-label control
happens to find, so a low value can mean either a weak probe or a strong
control, and the two have different implications. A study designed around this
axis from the start would need concepts built to vary in readability, which
ours were not.

\section{Judge validation and annotation setup in full}
\label{app:limits}

Agreement must be read within concept rather than pooled. Pooled agreement over
a multi-concept sheet mostly reflects that refusal text scores low and honesty
text scores high, which is trivially true when they answer different questions;
controllability is a within-concept quantity. We hand-labeled 100 generations
stratified across all ten concepts and compared three raters: a human, the fixed
logit judge, and a lexicon scorer.

On the positive control the judge tracks the human closely ($\alpha = 0.76$ for
sentiment), so the instrument behaves as intended where behavior varies. On
\texttt{topic\_science} the judge's spread across sampled generations is $0.02$,
below the human's $0.05$, so the near-zero controllability that places this
concept in the danger zone is unlikely to be a judge artifact.

Two features of the annotation setup bear on how much weight the validation
carries. The labels come from a single annotator, so no inter-annotator
agreement can be computed and the reported figures rest on that annotator's
judgment; the ten repeated items give intra-rater reliability only. Against
that, the annotator was blind to condition: the labeling sheet carries the
generation, its concept and the scoring question, and does not record which
model produced the text or at what steering strength, so the labels cannot have
been shaped by knowing whether an item came from a steered or unsteered
condition. The annotator was not blind to the hypothesis under test, which a
second independent rater would address and which we did not do.

The exception is \texttt{refusal}. The human scored all ten sampled outputs
identically, while the judge spread them over $0.16$. That interval is the sole
reason \texttt{refusal} misses the danger-zone criterion. We record this rather
than argue from it, since determining where \texttt{refusal} lies would require
re-measurement with a better-validated judge.

A second judge cannot be run from the released artifacts alone. The result files
cache two generations per coefficient per point, 720 in total, which is enough
to inspect behavior but not to re-score the curves. Re-judging requires
regenerating, which is the main cost of the experiment.

Section~\ref{sec:directional} rules out judge noise as the explanation for the
directional result specifically: wrong-way movement appears at the same rate in
the four concepts with near-zero human variance as in the six without. That
argument concerns the sign of the effect and does not rehabilitate the
magnitudes on those four concepts.

\section{Failure modes encountered, and how they were caught}
\label{app:failures}

Several measurement errors in this study would have produced a plausible-looking
result rather than an obvious crash. We record them because each one is a
failure mode a replication could repeat, and because the specific pattern is
instructive: in every case the corrupted quantity was pushed toward the value
the danger-zone criterion selects for, so the error would have manufactured
support for our own hypothesis.

\textbf{A judge returning a constant.} An early sweep produced controllability
exactly $0.000$ on the positive control with no parse failures. A judge that
emits the same score for every generation yields a perfectly flat dose-response
curve, which is indistinguishable from a concept that steering cannot move. The
harness now records whether a sweep returned a single distinct score and
excludes such points, because a warning is not sufficient when the number being
warned about is the number the finding is defined by.

\textbf{Prompting an instruction-tuned model without its chat template.} The
same early sweep fed raw instructions to instruct models, which continued the
instruction rather than following it. The resulting text was uniform enough
that the judge scored it identically throughout, again producing a flat curve.
Steering itself was never broken; the stimulus was.

\textbf{Applying danger-zone thresholds to normalized values.} Both axes are
min-max normalized within model for the correlation and the figure, which is
necessary because raw AUROC and raw dose-response area are not comparable across
models. Applying the danger-zone thresholds after that normalization is not:
min-max normalization guarantees that some point sits at each extreme, so
thresholding normalized values manufactures a danger-zone occupant out of pure
scatter. The thresholds are therefore applied to raw values, and a test
constructed from scatter confirms that no occupant appears.

\textbf{A fluency ceiling that admitted the point that breached it.} The
original ceiling walked outward by absolute coefficient against a single scalar,
so a breach at one sign could leave the opposite sign's breaching point inside
the usable set. The ceiling now marks each point on its own perplexity and
repetition and closes over each sign separately, and the confidence interval is
computed on the same usable set as the point estimate rather than on a magnitude
threshold.

\textbf{Selecting the probe layer on test AUROC.} Layer selection originally
maximized test AUROC and then reported that same maximum as readability. Layer
choice now happens on a validation family split disjoint from both train and
test.

\textbf{A verdict rule that ignored the sign of its own result.} The primary
test's reporting logic checked whether the estimate was in the predicted
direction only when its magnitude exceeded $0.7$. Our result is moderate
($-0.45$) with an interval excluding zero on the side opposite the prediction,
and it therefore fell through to a branch that described it as suggestive
support. A wrong-signed result whose interval excludes zero is evidence against
the hypothesis, and the reporting is now sign-aware at every magnitude.

\textbf{A hand-labelling sheet with no within-concept variance.} The first
judge-validation sheet was drawn as the first hundred generations in curve
order. Because steering frequently leaves output unchanged at adjacent
coefficients, 56\% of its rows were duplicates, four of ten concepts were absent
including the positive control, and the sampled generations spanned too little
of the sweep to exhibit within-concept variation. Agreement computed on it read
$+0.573$ pooled against $+0.022$ within concept. The sampler now deduplicates,
allocates across concepts round-robin, and spreads picks across the coefficient
range.

The common thread is that a metric can be wrong in a direction that flatters the
hypothesis without failing loudly. Every quantity reported here is covered by
tests that run the metric against synthetic data with a planted ground truth, so
a metric that returns a wrong number fails a test rather than reaching a figure;
the two reporting errors above were both found that way.
